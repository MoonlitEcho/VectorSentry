RECAP — PROBLEM STATEMENT
The Problem, Briefly Revisited
Production vector databases silently degrade in retrieval quality when a subset of documents is embedded by a different generation of the embedding model than the rest of the corpus. Cosine similarity is not comparable across generations, so the database keeps returning results — just quietly worse ones, with no exception ever thrown.

Real
Silent
Growing
OpenAI and Cohere have both shipped embedding updates that change internal geometry; teams re-embedding piecemeal create exactly this mix.
No exceptions, no alerts, no crash logs — the system looks healthy while quietly serving worse results.
20+ commercial vector-database systems have launched in the past five years (Pan et al., 2024) — every one exposed to this failure mode.

Since Review I:  we've moved from arguing this problem to measuring it. Our own 5,000-document benchmark (detailed ahead) now quantifies exactly how retrieval quality degrades as contamination rises — real data, not projection.

TESTING, VALIDATION & RISK ASSESSMENT
Testing, Validation & Risk Assessment
Real reliability issues found through destructive testing at full scale (5,000 docs) — and fixed before this review.

Shrinking-budget-pool stall
Budget was computed from the shrinking remaining-candidate count each tick, so the absolute budget eventually fell below the cost of even one fix — stalling the incident just short of resolution.
Fix:  Budget is now sized off a stable reference: the incident's initial flagged count, not the shrinking pool.

1

Repeated re-scheduling of fixed documents
A document already successfully remediated could be handed to the scheduler again if still flagged by residual noise, winning the zero-benefit tie-break every time and starving genuinely-contaminated documents of budget.
Fix:  Remediated documents are now tracked with a monotonic flag and excluded from all future scheduling rounds.

2

Infinite verification-retry loop
Escalating budget on verification failure only helps when scarcity IS the cause. When a fix shifted ranking for an unrelated query, escalating forever stalled the incident permanently at maximum budget.
Fix:  A MAX_CONSECUTIVE_FAILURES cap now defers the candidate for manual review instead of retrying forever.

3
These are regression tests in our own test suite today — each bug can never silently reappear.

### Notes:
This is genuine destructive testing, not a checkbox. Running the full system at five-thousand-document scale surfaced three real reliability bugs, none of which showed up in smaller smoke tests. Second, already-fixed documents could be repeatedly rescheduled, starving genuinely contaminated ones — fixed with a monotonic remediated flag. NOVELTY OF THE PROPOSED WORK
Novelty — Now Validated, Not Just Argued

First application (to our literature) of a Multiple-Choice Knapsack formulation to embedding-contamination remediation — now measured at 0.00% mean optimality gap (max 0.14%) against the exact solver.
A formal 3-way action space {Full Re-embed, Patch, Defer} where every reviewed prior work offers only a binary or single-fix choice — implemented and exercised across all 30 test scenarios.
A closed detect ? prioritize ? migrate ? verify loop with an automated safety gate — proven by 10 real, correct aborts in testing, not a theoretical claim.

Novelty
